# Research protocol — NBA officiating under public scrutiny

## Research questions

1. **Teams and players.** How are observed calls and errors that benefit or disadvantage a team or player distributed? How much of the variation remains after accounting for comparable opportunities and game context?
2. **Game outcomes.** Among independently verified errors with reconstructable consequences, how often does a rule-supported correction produce a material change in estimated win probability?
3. **First 46 minutes versus final two.** Do whistle frequency and independently assessed accuracy change in the final two minutes of regulation, when incidents in qualifying games can receive public L2M review?
4. **Stars versus non-stars.** For comparable contact, does favorable officiating differ by pregame star status? Does the difference vary across the two-minute boundary or by potential review exposure?

RQ3 is the primary pilot. RQ1 and RQ4 are descriptive or exploratory initially. RQ2 follows incident verification and validation of a suitable game-state model.

## RQ3 — primary video pilot

- Use regulation Q4 windows of equal duration immediately before and after 2:00 (proposed starting point: 4:00–2:00 and 2:00–0:00, with a single prespecified boundary convention). Treat broader first-46-versus-last-two summaries as descriptive. Analyze overtime separately if included later.
- Record season-specific L2M rules, realized report availability and the information available to officials at each event. Sample close games and consider comparable games without reports. Distinguish actual retrospective coverage from potential scrutiny at the time of the decision. Avoid treating future score paths or report publication as pre-decision exposure.
- Use the existing 48-case L2M queue for calibration and keep it separate from the formal sample. The proposed feasibility target is 10 full-game sources, 150–250 candidate incidents, and two independent reviewers. Sample video windows as well as PBP candidates to capture uncalled contact. Record inclusion rules and source stream.
- Use a common codebook in both windows. Log period, clock, score margin, possession, contact category, visibility, whistle, independent rule judgment and adjudication. Hide L2M grades and the other reviewer's labels during independent review. Record when visible clock or broadcast context prevents masking.
- Report whistles per observed possession and error rates among adjudicable sampled opportunities, separating wrong calls from missed calls. Also report locatability, pre-consensus agreement, indeterminate rates and review time. Use game-level uncertainty when sample size supports it.
- Compare within games and assess sensitivity to window width, score margin, possession, bonus and intentional fouling. Late-game tactics change near 2:00. A threshold difference is an association unless a credible comparison design supports a scrutiny effect. Treat the pilot as feasibility work.

## RQ1 and RQ4 — team, player and star patterns

- Start with L2M counts by benefiting and disadvantaged team or player, separating correct and incorrect calls and non-calls. Document unknown participants and report denominators. Official L2M rates describe selected reviewed incidents.
- For video-based comparisons, define comparable contact opportunities, relevant offensive or defensive roles and favorable outcomes before coding. Account for possession involvement, contact type, score, time and home/away context. Report sample sizes and uncertainty rather than ranking sparse groups.
- Prespecify a pregame star definition, such as a prior-season All-Star selection, and test sensitivity to alternatives. Compare like roles and contact opportunities. Test a star-by-window interaction only after sample size and coverage are adequate.

## RQ2 — consequence of verified errors

- Require independent incident verification and a rule-supported alternative ruling. Reconstruct pre-decision clock, score, possession, foul/bonus and free-throw context.
- Compare observed and corrected scenarios at a common decision point, using explicit assumptions for free throws, continuation and subsequent possession. Validate the model in comparable game states before using it for these contrasts.
- Prespecify a materiality threshold in percentage points and report sensitivity to that threshold and to plausible scenario branches. Report the fraction among eligible verified errors, plus counts and reasons for unresolved or excluded cases. A modeled win-probability difference does not establish that the actual winner would have changed.
